Your Personalized Recommendations

Your scores reveal someone who is moving through life with strong forward momentum and a solid sense of self, yet you're carrying some weight in how you show up in relationships and express who you really are. With an overall alignment of 82, you've done meaningful work on accepting yourself and clarifying your core beliefs, but there's a noticeable gap between your internal clarity and how authentically you're connecting with others. You're highly oriented toward the future and have made peace with much of your past, but the present moment—particularly in relational dynamics—is where your energy isn't flowing as freely.

Your lowest score in Relationships and Authenticity matters because this is where theory meets practice. Research in social psychology consistently shows that authentic self-expression in relationships doesn't just improve connection quality—it actually reinforces the self-acceptance work you've already done. When there's a disconnect between who we know ourselves to be and how we show up with others, it creates a subtle but persistent drain on our energy and confidence. This isn't about fixing something broken; it's about closing the gap between the inner work you've completed and the outer expression that will make it real and sustainable.

Start a practice of naming one true thing in conversations where you'd normally stay surface-level, even if it's small—this builds the muscle of authentic expression without requiring you to overhaul every interaction at once.

Identify one relationship where you feel the biggest gap between your internal experience and what you actually share, and write out what you'd say if there were no risk involved. You don't have to send it, but getting it out of your head and onto paper clarifies what authenticity would actually look like for you.

Set a weekly check-in with yourself where you ask: "Where did I edit myself this week, and what was I protecting?" This isn't about judgment—it's about gathering data on your patterns so you can make conscious choices instead of automatic ones.

Choose one recurring social situation and experiment with sharing your actual opinion or preference instead of deferring or accommodating. Track how it feels and what actually happens, because often our fear of the consequences is larger than the reality.
